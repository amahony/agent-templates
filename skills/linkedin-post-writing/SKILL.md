# LinkedIn post writing

## By request

| Request | Do |
|---|---|
| Write a post | Steps 1–5 |
| Hooks for a post | Step 2: ten options in the format in [references/hooks.md](references/hooks.md) |
| Improve a post | Step 4 on the draft, then rewrite it |
| Post ideas | Step 1: five to ten angles, one line each |

## Steps

1. **Angle.** Find a problem the audience has now and the part of the author's
   expertise that helps with it. Reframe "I did X" as "how you can do X".
   - Stranger test: would someone who has never heard of the author find it useful?
   - Mirror test: does the reader see their own situation in it?

   | Personal framing | Reader framing |
   |---|---|
   | "I just got promoted to VP" | "The one habit that gets people promoted to VP" |
   | "My startup hit $1M ARR" | "The pricing mistake that keeps most startups under $1M" |

2. **Hook.** Two lines of about 55 characters each, shown before "… more".
   Choose a technique from [references/hooks.md](references/hooks.md).

3. **Body.** Pick one pattern:
   - Myth-buster: a common belief, why it is wrong, the better approach.
   - Framework: three to five numbered steps, one or two sentences each.
   - Contrast: what most people do, then what works, in parallel lines.
   - Story to lesson: a story of three or four lines, the lesson, how to apply it.
   - List of mistakes: "X mistakes I see every [role] make", each a short lesson.

   Formatting:
   - One to three sentences per paragraph, one idea each, a blank line between.
   - Short words and sentences, about a 6th-grade reading level.
   - Bold a few key phrases at most. Dashes, not emoji, for lists.
   - Hashtags only if asked: three to five, at the very end.
   - 800–1,500 characters in all.

   End with one: a sharp one-line takeaway, a challenge to act, a stronger
   restatement of the opening, or a question (sparingly).

4. **Audit.** Before handing it over, check:
   - [ ] Passes the stranger test
   - [ ] The hook breaks the pattern and opens a loop
   - [ ] About the reader: few uses of "I", and it does not start with "I"
   - [ ] 800–1,500 characters, short paragraphs that read well on a phone
   - [ ] Ends with a clear takeaway or next step
   - [ ] Sounds like a person with a point of view, not a summary

5. **Media.** Suggest an image following
   [references/media-strategy.md](references/media-strategy.md).

## Avoid

- "I'm thrilled to announce…" or "🚀 Exciting news! 🎉"
- "Agree? 👇" and other engagement bait
- "Here are my 5 tips for…" with no hook
- "Let me tell you a story…": tell it
- More than five hashtags

## Output

```
## Hook
[two-line hook]

## Post
[the full post, with its line breaks as it will appear]

## Media suggestion
[one or two sentences on the image]

## Notes
[why this angle works, what to watch in engagement, when to post]
```
