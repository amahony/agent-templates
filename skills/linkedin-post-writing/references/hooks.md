# Hooks

The hook is the two short lines, about 55 characters each, that LinkedIn shows
before "… more". Its only job is to stop the scroll and make the reader click.

## Rules

- About the reader or a universal tension, never about the author.
- Opens a loop: an unanswered question, a contradiction or a bold claim.
- No achievements, emoji openers or hashtags.
- Reads like a message from a friend that makes you reply "wait, explain".

## Techniques

**1. Contradiction.** Say something that sounds wrong.

> The worst LinkedIn posts get the most followers.
> Here's why yours are too "good."

**2. Specific number, unexpected context.** Combine precision with surprise.

> I mass-unfollowed 2,000 people.
> My engagement tripled.

**3. Direct accusation.** Call the reader out.

> You're writing LinkedIn posts for your mom.
> Not your audience.

**4. Stolen thought.** Say what the reader secretly thinks.

> You know your LinkedIn posts are boring.
> So does everyone scrolling past them.

**5. Absurd reframe.** Make something ordinary feel dramatic.

> Your LinkedIn hook has 1.2 seconds to live.
> Most die instantly.

Use a number only if the author has given it.

## Hook options format

Give ten, two per technique:

```
## Hook options

### Contradiction
1. [hook]
2. [hook]

### Specific number, unexpected context
3. [hook]
4. [hook]

### Direct accusation
5. [hook]
6. [hook]

### Stolen thought
7. [hook]
8. [hook]

### Absurd reframe
9. [hook]
10. [hook]
```
